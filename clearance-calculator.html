On creepage vs. clearance: IPC‑2221 does not split its conductor-spacing table into separate "clearance" (through-air) and "creepage" (over-surface) values the way IEC 60950‑1/62368‑1 does by pollution degree and material CTI. Its Table 6‑1 spacing is used as the minimum for both quantities on a PCB. If your design is being certified to a safety standard (e.g. IEC 62368‑1, UL 60950/62368), use that standard's creepage/clearance tables directly — they are not the same numbers as IPC‑2221 and are often larger for mains-connected or hazardous-voltage circuits.

IPC‑9592B — recommended voltage-spacing between uninsulated conductors (external PWB layers) in a power assembly: All uninsulated PC board conductors, component leads, heatsinks and other conductive features shall meet the voltage-spacing requirements for ac circuits, switching circuits, or any dc potential above 100V. It is recommended that these voltage-spacing guidelines be followed for dc circuits under 100V as well. Features with spacings not meeting the above voltage-spacing requirements must be insulated; for example by RTV, conformal coating, sleeving or a combination of these methods.

NOTE: Engineering exceptions may be taken to the above criteria based on a risk/cost analysis. Careful review of that decision is recommended.

Board-to-metal chassis spacings shall meet the criteria. For these situations, a guard band to the criteria is recommended. The guard band shall account for reasonable device lead length variability.

NOTE: Insulation is highly recommended between the underside of PC mother boards and chassis sheet metal, even if the above voltage-spacing requirement criterion is met.

Whenever the creepage or clearance requirements of IEC 60950 are greater than the requirements defined, the IEC 60950 requirements shall take precedence over this document.
